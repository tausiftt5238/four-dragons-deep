Art reference · 24 demons · South Asian folk-horror

Drawing the Afflictions

Somewhere to look while you draw. Every demon in the registry paired with two or three real touchstones and a note on the one thing its silhouette has to do. The references lean on subcontinental folk-horror — bhoot, churel, pret, pey — because those are beings people warded against rather than prayed to, which is the rule this roster was built on.

One shape, one violation

At sprite size the question is never what creature. It is what survives being shrunk to a thumbnail. Everything below is chosen for that.

Silhouette firstFill it solid black. If you cannot tell what it is, no amount of interior detail will save it.

One familiar shapeA dog, a person, a doorway. The viewer needs something to recognise before you break it.

One violationExactly one thing wrong — a furnace for a jaw, no face above the mouth, feet on backwards. Two makes noise.

Four to six coloursCount them. Low-tier demons in the games you admire almost never shade; they use flat fills and let shape do the work.

One saturated thingEverything else desaturated. The bright spot is where the eye lands, so spend it on the violation.

Head on, one frameThese are seen face-forward at a fixed distance. No three-quarter poses, no foreshortening to solve.

The shared grammar

Subcontinental folk-horror has a consistent visual vocabulary, and most of it is subtractive — something missing or reversed rather than added. That suits pixel art, where you have very few pixels to add anything with.

Feet on backwards

The most portable tell in the subcontinent — bhoot, churel and the Nepali kichkandi all share it. Costs you four pixels and reads instantly.

No shadow, no reflection

The dead cast neither. If every other sprite gets a contact shadow, withholding one is a whole characterisation.

Unbound hair

Braided or tied in life; loose, long and unkempt in death. A silhouette made almost entirely of hair reads as female and as dead at once.

Feet off the ground

Not floating dramatically — hovering an inch, so the wrongness is easy to miss. Draw the gap, not the pose.

The trees

Peepal, banyan and tamarind are where things live. Anything hanging, upside down or half-grown into bark belongs to this family.

Ash and the burning ground

Grey on grey, matte, no highlights. A palette that is nearly all one value is a legitimate choice for the right demon.

The wards

Iron, turmeric, mustard seed, a lemon-and-chilli string over a door. Yellow and iron-grey are the colours of protection — useful to withhold, or to corrupt.

The threshold

The dehri is a real line you do not cross at dusk. A demon framed inside a doorway is already half-designed.

Draw these first

already in the game · waiting on sprites

Hushmouth

A human jaw on its own, hung at head height across a doorway, wired shut through the teeth.

  • North IndiaThe lemon-and-chilli string hung above a shop door to turn away the evil eye. Hushmouth occupies exactly that spot and does the opposite job — the format is familiar, the object is wrong.
  • BengalNishi, the voice that calls your name once at night, which you must never answer. Hushmouth is its inverse: a mouth that has been stopped.
  • MaterialRusted picture wire on bone. Two materials, two values, nothing else.

SilhouetteOne object floating at head height with nothing above or below it. A crescent, crossed by straight lines. The easiest thing in the roster to draw — start here and let it set your palette and line weight for everything after.

The Held Breath

A room's worth of air pulled into the outline of someone standing. Dust never settles inside it.

  • BengalNishi again — a presence known only by its effect. Nobody describes what it looks like, which is the point.
  • Pan-IndianThe shadowless bhoot. Here you go further: draw nothing but the absence.
  • MaterialHeat shimmer over a courtyard in May.

SilhouetteDo not draw the figure. Draw a speckle field of dust and cut a person-shaped hole in it. The outline is the only line on the sprite, and it should be the dust stopping rather than a drawn edge.

Vacancy

A tall adult silhouette in a lit doorway, backlit hard, with no light and no room behind it.

  • Pan-IndianThe dehri — the threshold you do not cross at dusk, and do not stand in. The whole design is a taboo made literal.
  • BengalSkondhokata, the headless one, for how a pure silhouette carries a figure with no interior detail at all.
  • MaterialA doorway photographed against a window.

SilhouetteSolid black on a bright rectangle — the highest contrast in the game. The single violation: its edges do not match the doorframe it stands in. Offset them by two pixels and let the player notice late.

The Tenant

A heavy seated figure built from years of nesting material, with a face worn smooth into the front of it.

  • MaharashtraMunjya — the spirit of a boy who died before his thread ceremony, who takes up residence in a peepal and will not be moved. Tenancy is the whole idea.
  • GermanicThe Mare that sits on a sleeper's chest. Worth one look for the posture: settled, patient, in no hurry.
  • MaterialA termite mound, or an abandoned wasp nest — layered, papery, the colour of dust.

SilhouetteA heavy triangle, widest at the base, limbs folded away underneath. The face is a dent rather than a feature. This is your boss: it should be the only sprite whose outline is stable and symmetrical, because nothing about it is going anywhere.

Tier I

floors 1–2 · answerable to a blade or a flame

Nagwort

Knee-high, woven out of chewed fingernails and pulled hair, always facing away.

  • Pan-IndianThe rule that cut hair and nail clippings must be burned or buried, never left where someone could pick them up. Nagwort is what that rule is afraid of.
  • North IndiaChurel's matted, unbound hair — for texture, not for the figure.
  • MaterialKeratin. Dry, yellowish, slightly translucent at the edges.

SilhouetteSmall and hunched, facing away from the viewer. The whole read is edge noise — a ragged, hairy outline around a solid core. No face to draw, which is why it is the second-easiest after Hushmouth.

Thrum

A human outline packed out of grey moths, all beating out of sync.

  • BengalAleya, the marsh lights that lead fishermen off the path — many small things reading as one presence.
  • MaterialMoths at a tube light on the first wet night of the monsoon. You already know this silhouette.
  • TamilPey, the ghoul of burning grounds, for the grey-on-grey palette.

SilhouetteA person-shape whose edge is ragged and whose interior is full of holes. Keep the outline recognisably human or the whole thing reads as static.

The Tallyman

A bookkeeper with far too many fingers, each ending in a stylus, scoring marks into his own forearms.

  • Pan-IndianThe village munshi and his bahi-khata — the red cloth-bound ledger, the folded knees, the posture of a man who has been sitting a long time.
  • SanskriticPishach's roped, bulging veins — use it on the forearms only.
  • MaterialInk on skin. Old marks faded, new ones wet.

SilhouetteNarrow and vertical; all the information is in the hands. Draw them two sizes too large — at this resolution an anatomically correct hand is four pixels and says nothing.

Grinner

A body in good clothes that do not fit, with nothing at all above the hand-widened mouth.

  • Pan-IndianThe bhoot with backward feet — the classic borrowed-body tell. This is the demon to spend it on.
  • MaterialA tailor's dummy in a shuttered shop; clothes with nobody's shape in them.
  • CompositionThe top of the head simply absent, cut flat, as if cropped.

SilhouetteAn ordinary standing human — deliberately the most normal outline in the game. The violation is a flat horizontal cut where a face should be. Put the feet on backwards and let that be the second read.

The Understudy

It has a face, and the face is very nearly right. A seam runs along the jaw where it was fitted on.

  • North IndiaChurel, beautiful from the front and wrong from behind — take the principle, not the figure.
  • TamilMohini pisasu, which lures by wearing a borrowed face.
  • MaterialA papier-mâché festival mask worn a size too loose.

SilhouetteEntirely normal — it has to be, or the design fails. Everything lives in one line along the jaw. If that seam is not legible at final size, move it to the hairline instead; do not thicken it.

Tier II

floors 2–3 · the first drains

Gristlebone

A knot of knuckles and joints with no limbs between them, walking on eight small hands.

  • SanskriticPishach and the bones of the burning ground — the raw material, nothing more.
  • MaterialA knob of ginger root. Lumpen, pale, branching wrongly.
  • MovementA crab, for how many legs read at small size when they are all the same length.

SilhouetteA lumpy mass on many thin legs — radial rather than bilateral, which makes it the only sprite in the roster with no front. Odd number of visible legs; an even split looks like a diagram.

Meatlace

A lattice of tendon strung corner to corner across a doorway, dragging itself by contracting.

  • Pan-IndianThe toran or bandanwar strung across a doorway to welcome. This is that shape and that placement, made of the wrong thing — the strongest single inversion available to you.
  • MaterialDried sinew; a spiderweb in a room nobody opens.
  • CompositionThe doorway is part of the sprite. Draw the frame.

SilhouetteNot a creature at all — a rectangle crossed by lines. The only sprite that should read as architecture. Keep the strands slack, not taut; tension makes it look designed rather than grown.

Hollowmarrow

Very tall, very thin, skin stretched over an absence. Your breath fogs in front of it.

  • NepalKichkandi — tall, thin, backward-footed, and cold.
  • SanskriticPret, the hungry dead: needle throat, swollen middle, limbs like sticks. a rebirth state, not a figure of worship
  • MaterialA scarecrow at the end of the season.

SilhouetteHeight is the whole design — make it at least two heads taller than anything else on screen, and thin enough that the tallness reads as wrongness rather than strength. Fog only at the mouth.

Cinderjaw

Dog-shaped, except the lower jaw is a hanging furnace door that will not close.

  • TamilKollivay pey, the fire-mouthed one — a mouth that is a source of light.
  • BengalBegho bhoot, the ghost of someone taken by a tiger, for the animal build.
  • MaterialA street dog's proportions: high shoulder, low head, thin flank.

SilhouetteA plain dog profile, which the player will read in one glance. Then one bright rectangle where the jaw should be — the only saturated colour on the sprite, and the only light source in the frame.

Kindleweep

Child-sized and made of wet ash. It strikes itself trying to catch, and never does.

  • MaharashtraMunjya again — a boy who died too early, pitiable and dangerous in the same breath.
  • TamilKuzhandai pey, the child ghost.
  • MaterialA cold hearth the morning after. Damp ash holds a shape and then slumps.

SilhouetteSmall, rounded, head too large for the body — child proportions do most of the work. Grey on grey throughout, so the one reaching arm is the only thing that moves the eye.

Tier III

floors 3–4 · all six elements, and the first nulls

Sillage

The shape of a woman who has just left the room, held up by nothing but her own perfume.

  • KeralaThe folk yakshi, announced by jasmine before she is seen, waiting by a palm at night. The best match in the roster. yakshis are venerated elsewhere — this is the Malayali horror figure
  • BengalAleya, for a presence that is only ever at the edge of sight.
  • MaterialThe dent a body leaves in a bedsheet.

SilhouetteDraw her mostly missing: densest at the contour, empty through the middle. If you can fill her in solid and still read a woman, you have drawn too much. This is the hardest sprite in the roster — leave it until your palette is settled.

Ossuara

A cathedral of ribs on femur stilts, with a nest of small singing skulls where a heart would hang.

  • MaterialA bamboo scaffold lashed at the joints — the structure is the design, and bamboo reads better at small size than bone does.
  • MaterialAn upturned boat's ribs on a riverbank.
  • SanskriticBurning-ground bone imagery, kept to the palette rather than the pose.

SilhouetteA tall arch on thin legs. The negative space between the ribs is the whole read — this is the one sprite where the holes matter more than the fill. Keep the skull nest small and central so it does not compete.

Fenwrack

Waterlogged past having a shape, dragging a net of everything it has ever drowned.

  • BengalMecho bhoot — the pond ghost that wants your catch. Ponds, nets and a smell of fish are its whole world.
  • MaterialWater hyacinth: matted green, clinging, covering everything at the waterline.
  • MaterialSwelling. Soft edges, no joints visible.

SilhouetteThe body has no clear edge, so the net is what makes it legible — draw that crisp and geometric against a soft, formless mass. Contrast of edge quality, not of value.

Sootmother

Swollen with chimney soot, breathing out infants of smoke that come apart before they land.

  • North IndiaChurel — the woman who died in childbirth, the most feared figure in the whole tradition. Everything Sootmother is doing, Churel is about.
  • BengalPetni in a tamarind tree, for the hunched, low, waiting posture.
  • MaterialThe inside of a chulha flue. Matte black that eats light.

SilhouetteHeavy and low, wider than tall. The infants are the only motion — keep them faint and half-formed so they read as smoke rather than as a second sprite competing for attention.

Verdigrease

Shaped like an angel once, gone green with corrosion, wings oxidised into one solid sheet.

  • MaterialVerdigris on brass — old vessels, door fittings, a handle nobody has turned in years. Take the material, not any figure it was cast as.
  • MaterialA metal thing left in the monsoon: streaked, pitted, weeping down its own surface.
  • CompositionAn arm raised and stuck there. The pose is the story.

SilhouetteOne solid wedge for the fused wings, with a single arm frozen out of it. Do not draw feathers — they have not been feathers for a long time. Green is your only hue; everything else is value.

Tier IV

floors 4+ · three of the five throw something back at you

Static Choir

Three faces sharing one throat, each singing a note a quarter-tone off the others.

  • NepalThe carving of a Lakhey mask — the bulging eyes, the fixed grin, the way a mask reads at distance. the Lakhey dance is living festival practice — take the carving, not the figure
  • MaterialA loose filling. The copper taste before the sound.
  • CompositionThree heads fanned from one neck, perfectly even.

SilhouetteA single column that splits into three at the top. Symmetry is the horror here, so make it exact — this is the only sprite that should be mirror-perfect, and the evenness is what will unsettle.

Coldsmith

He works a forge that runs backwards, taking heat out of whatever is put in. Frostbitten black to the wrist.

  • Pan-IndianThe village lohar at his bellows — the crouch, the tongs, the low anvil.
  • Pan-IndianIron as the ward against bhoot. He is the protection itself, running in reverse.
  • MaterialFrostbite. Black at the fingers, purple further up, and no blood beyond.

SilhouetteHunched over a bright rectangle that casts no light — the forge glows and the room stays dark, which is the whole joke. His hands should be the darkest pixels on the sprite, darker than the outline.

Gallowglass

A hanged silhouette assembled out of window glass, turning slowly on nothing.

  • Pan-IndianMirrors turned to the wall or covered after a death — the reflection taboo, made into a body.
  • MaterialA broken pane still in its frame. Straight fractures, no curves anywhere.
  • CompositionWhat it reflects is not the room you are in.

SilhouetteA human outline built entirely from straight-edged shards — ban curves from this sprite completely, including the head. It should look like it would cut you, before you know what it is.

The Long Patient

A body under a sheet on a gurney, always at the far end of whichever corridor you turn down. Too many feet.

  • MaterialA covered shape on a bier. a real funeral image — keep it to cloth and form, not ritual
  • BengalSkondhokata for how a shape with no visible detail still reads as a person.
  • CompositionThe drape does the anatomy. Every fold implies something underneath.

SilhouetteThe only horizontal in the entire roster — low, long, flat-topped, which makes it unmistakable among twenty-three standing figures. The sheet never moves, so keep the outline perfectly still. Count the feet and make sure the player can too.

Mourncoil

A column of black hair and mourning veil wound into a spiral two storeys high, with a woman still turning somewhere inside.

  • BengalShakchunni and Petni — white sari, conch bangles, hair unbound to the waist. The single closest cultural match in the roster.
  • Pan-IndianHair loosened in mourning, which in life is the one time it is not tied.
  • MaterialA coil of rope on a jetty. Same logic, no face.

SilhouetteA tall spiral — one shape, one texture, no face anywhere. The white of the veil should be the only light value; let it spiral upward so the eye travels and never finds a head to look at.

Where the line sits

The roster's founding rule is that nothing in it is worshipped, and that rule survives references intact as long as you take how a thing is drawn and leave its identity behind. Silhouette, palette, material, proportion — all portable. A name, an attribute, an iconography that belongs to a living devotional tradition is not.

Two practical cautions. Searching for “Indian demons” will hand you deities labelled as demons by whoever wrote the page — asuras, rakshasas and yakshas are antagonists in some texts and objects of devotion in others, so the label is not a reliable guide. And a handful of folk figures are venerated regionally even where they read as horror elsewhere; the three flagged above are the ones in this list where that applies.

The safest seam, and the richest one, is the bhoot-pret family proper — the restless dead. Nobody has ever built a shrine to a churel. People hang a lemon and two chillies over the door and hope she passes.

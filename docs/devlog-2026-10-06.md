# Devlog: off the Play Store, onto itch.io, and on to Steam

A week ago I was getting ready to put the game on Google Play. I had a store
listing, a feature graphic, a trailer and a signed release build, and testers
were signing up for the closed test. Then I stopped. Google wants twelve
testers playing for fourteen days before a new developer can even apply for a
public release, and the forms and checks around it kept growing. For a free
game with no ads, it wasn't worth the effort.

So the phone version goes up here on itch.io instead, under a new name, and
the real focus from now on is a PC version for Steam.

## Four Dragons Deep Portable

The phone version is now **Four Dragons Deep Portable**. It's the same game
I've been writing about in these devlogs, played upright with one hand. You
can play it two ways:

- **In the browser**, here on the page. That's the main game: twenty floors,
  four dragons, and the Necromancer under them.
- **As an Android APK.** That's everything, including the Abyss.

The Abyss stays out of the web version. That's the mode you unlock by beating
the game. On the web, beating the Necromancer ends with the walk home and the
credits.

## What changed since the last devlog

The Play Store work wasn't wasted. Most of that week went into the game itself.

**Balance.** I wrote a bot that plays a run on its own and reports where it
got stuck. Its first findings:

- An Imp or a Demon on floor 1 could kill a level 1 hero.
- The heals fell behind in the deep bands.
- The Thunder Dragon was a wall.

Heals scale further now, wardens and dragons pay twice the EXP and gold, and
dragons get two casts of MP back each phase so they don't run dry halfway
through. The Thunder Dragon stays a wall until you bring an Attack Mirror and
a Magic Mirror, and that one's on purpose.

**Armour means something.** Armoured monsters shrug off blades: they resist
physical hits in the first two bands and reflect them in the last two. To get
through, there's **Pierce**, a physical skill that ignores armour, sold from
floor 11. There are also twelve layered armours that resist two or three
elements and leave others open, so what you wear is a choice and not just a
bigger number.

**Ambushes.** A pack can now get the jump on you, depending on your agility
and luck against theirs. If you hate that, the **Sentry's Whistle** (500 gold,
first shop) means nothing ever does. The **Wellspring Charm** (100 gold) gives
MP back as you walk.

**Light has casters.** The Eyeball, the Demoness and the Warlock throw light
now, so the gear that resists it finally earns its slot.

**Deep supplies.** From floor 16 the orbs sell the Super Potion, Hi-Ether and
Elixir, plus the Potion Cauldron and Ether Fountain, which heal the whole party.

**The Abyss.** Beat the game and the captain sends your hero back down into
an endless dungeon:

- One life: a death wipes the run.
- Every monster rises in an element of its own and can be recruited that way.
- Wardens and dragons roam the floors.
- New gear waits every five floors.

There's a **Records** screen with your lifetime numbers: play time, deepest
floor, dragons slain. And the ending has your knight walking home with whoever
is still in his party.

## The Steam version

This is where I'm putting my time now. The phone layout doesn't work on a wide
screen, so the PC version is a separate interface over the same game:

- **Exploring:** the map down the left in its own window, the dungeon on the
  right, and your party along the bottom of the view.
- **Fights:** Final Fantasy style. Your party stands in a ring on the right,
  the monsters in a ring on the left, and whoever's turn it is walks on the
  spot. Every floor has its own battle room in that floor's stone, so the
  fight happens on solid ground and not in front of a corridor wall.
- **Menus:** like Digital Devil Saga. Left and right switch between Skills,
  Items, Talk and the rest, and up and down move through the list. Talking to
  a monster opens a window stacked on top of the menu.
- **The menu and the orb** slide in from the left and push the map across to
  the right edge. Closing them slides it all back.
- **Controls:** the whole game runs on six buttons: the four directions, O for
  yes and X for no. Every one can be rebound, on a keyboard or a controller,
  and the window stretches to fit a Steam Deck's taller screen.

There's no release date yet. I still need to playtest the whole thing on the
new layout. I'll post here when there's something to show.

Thanks for playing.

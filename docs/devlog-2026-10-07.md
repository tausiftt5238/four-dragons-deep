# Devlog: dragons you can talk to

The game is called Four Dragons Deep, and until this week that's all the
dragons there were: one at the bottom of each band, and none of them would
listen to a word you said. Now the dungeon is full of them, and every one of
these new ones can be talked into joining you.

## Seventeen dragons

The dragon art I bought for the four bosses came with a lot more in it,
including dragons at different ages. They're in the game now, sorted by age,
so the deeper you go the older they get:

- **Babies** on floors 1 to 4: Brass, Copper, Green, White and Iron.
- **Young** dragons on floors 6 to 9: Red, Brass, Copper and Silver.
- **Juveniles** on floors 11 to 14: Bronze, Black, Mercury and Multihued.
- **Mature** dragons on floors 16 to 19: Bronze, Black, Blue and Iron.

They're ordinary monsters, not bosses. They come in packs with everything
else, and you can talk to them, bribe them and bind them like any demon.

Each one breathes what its colour says. Red and Brass breathe fire, White,
Silver and Blue breathe ice, Copper and Bronze breathe thunder, and Green and
Black breathe dark and poison. Iron is a brawler with no breath at all. The
Mercury dragon throws ice and light at the whole party and turns ice back on
whoever cast it. The Multihued one carries fire, ice and thunder at once and
shrugs off all three, but a blade or the dark goes straight through it.

They also get tougher as they grow up. A baby has a plain weakness and
nothing else. A young dragon can't be hurt by its own element. A juvenile
starts resisting blades. A mature dragon acts twice a turn and heals from its
own element.

There's no Young Blue Dragon. That art is already the Ice Dragon on floor 5,
and meeting a smaller copy of the first boss in an ordinary pack felt wrong.

## Dragons flinch now

The dragon art only came with an idle animation, so the four bosses never
reacted when you hit them, apart from a shake. Every other monster flashes a
red outline when it's hurt. Now all the dragons do too, the bosses included.

## The Abyss is readable

In the Abyss every monster comes up in an element of its own, and its colours
used to change to match. The trouble was how: the old effect painted the whole
sprite in two shades of one colour, and a bug in it darkened everything on top
of that. A Frost Orc was a dark blue smudge.

Now only the monster's main colour changes. A red demon stays red as an Ember
Demon and turns blue as a Frost Demon, and its skin, armour, eyes and weapon
stay as they were drawn. Mostly grey monsters, like the skeletons, get a light
wash of the element instead. You can tell what everything is again.

The web version doesn't have the Abyss, so this one is for the APK and the
Steam version.

## Levelling doesn't stall any more

Each level used to cost about 20% more than the last, compounding, while the
EXP monsters pay only grows with the square of their level. Early on you
couldn't tell. By the floors under the Void Dragon, a level-35 hero needed
around seventy kills for one level, and the Necromancer sat fifteen levels
out of reach.

Now a level costs about ten kills of a monster at your own level, from the
first floor to the last. At level 35 that's 6,220 EXP instead of about 48,000.
Your save carries over: it keeps the share of the current level you'd already
earned.

## Also in this one

- **The Tide Stone**, for 500 gold, gives the whole party some MP back every
  round of a fight.
- **Defend** can be used turn after turn now, and it catches a little HP and
  MP back while you hold.
- **Demons can run away** from a fight now, the way you can.
- **HP Leech and MP Leech** can be learned when a bound demon levels up, so
  they're not only for bats and blood monsters any more.
- You can't walk while the orb or a chest is open. Before, a swipe could take a
  step behind the window.

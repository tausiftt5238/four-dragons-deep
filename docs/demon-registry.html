The Affliction Registry

Twenty demons written to replace the fantasy roster. None of them is a god, a devil, or anything anyone has ever prayed to — they are invented whole, and shaped as afflictions: things that nest in a mind and can be diagnosed from the outside. Every entry's appearance is written to give away its affinity chart, so a player who looks carefully can work out what to hit it with before spending a turn finding out.

Affinity states, and what each costs in press turns

WEAK Double damage. Costs half an icon — the reason to scout.
RESIST Half damage. Still a full icon. Wasteful, not fatal.
NULL No damage, and it costs two icons. Punishing.
REPEL Thrown back at the caster. Your phase ends on the spot.
DRAIN It heals for the damage. Your phase ends on the spot.

Three things baked into the roster

Alignment tells you which Talk option lands. It is not flavour — it maps one-to-one onto the four negotiation personalities already in the code, with no exceptions anywhere in these twenty. Learn the rule once and every demon's alignment is a hint:

  • LawAnswers to Logic. Argue that the arithmetic is wrong.
  • ChaosAnswers to Gain. Ask what it actually gets out of this.
  • Neutral · LightAnswers to Flatter or Safety. It wants to be wanted.
  • Neutral · DarkAnswers to Survival. It would rather not be here either.

Talk difficulty stops at 3 on purpose. The trust formula is max(0, randi()%4 + 2 − difficulty) against a threshold of 4 over two rounds, so at difficulty 4 a single round can never gain more than 1 and recruitment becomes arithmetically impossible. Difficulty 3 is already a 6% coin-flip. Three demons here refuse outright instead — a mindless thing that will not talk is honest, an impossible check is not.

Every appearance gives away its chart. Soaked things do not burn. Glass cuts the hand that strikes it. A thing reaching for your torch should not be handed fire. This is what makes the roster diagnosable rather than memorisable — the player reads the room, not the wiki.

# Devlog — the dungeon turned to stone

Until this week the dungeon was drawn the way the old grid crawlers drew it:
every wall an outline, every floor a square of lines, dark fill behind. It read
fine. It never looked like a place.

It's stone now.

## Walls, floors, a ceiling

The walls are coursed blocks, the floor is big flagstones, and the ceiling is
the same slabs laid the other way and darker, so looking up and looking down
don't show you the same thing mirrored. None of it is a texture. A shader works
the stone out from where each pixel sits in the world and snaps it to a coarse
grid, so it comes out as pixel art at the same chunkiness as the sprites
instead of a smooth 3D surface sitting next to them.

Each band's rock takes a little of that band's colour: cold on floor 1, sandy
further down, burnt near the bottom. You can tell roughly how deep you are from
the walls alone.

The outlines are still there, just faint. The grid on the floor is how you
count steps, and I didn't want to lose that.

## Torches

Iron brackets with a flame in each, a few steps apart, and they hang in the
same places every time a floor is rebuilt.

They aren't real lights. The web version lights any one mesh from at most eight
lights, and the whole floor's walls are one mesh, so a corridor of real
torches would have lost most of them. The stone shader adds up the nearest
dozen torches and your own lamp itself, flicker included.

## The orb loses its plinth

Last devlog the orb got a plinth so you could tell which tile it was on. With
flagstones under it, the square of light on its own tile does that job alone.
It floats again.

## A door and a way down

The two things you walk the whole floor for were the last things still drawn in
lines: a violet slab with bands across it, and a flight of glowing green edges.

**The door** is iron-strapped planks now, set in a doorway cut through the
wall, with a dressed stone frame and the bottom step showing under it as a
threshold. The lock is a dark iron plate with the key's shape glowing violet
inside it, and that violet falls on the stone around the door. From down the
corridor, the glow is how you know what's at the end.

**The stairs** were harder. You stand at eye height, so every tread is almost
edge-on, and six stone risers of the same colour merge into one wall. The first
try looked exactly like that: a brick wall with a green tint. What fixed it was
making each riser exactly one course of stone, with a bright worn nosing along
its top and shadow at its foot. Six light lines stepping back into the dark
read as a flight straight away. Green light pours down from the top onto the
corridor mouth.

Both had OmniLights on them before. They never did anything. The stone ignores
real lights (see torches), so the glow had been decoration on a decoration.
The shader carries one coloured glow now, violet while the door is shut and
green once it's open.

## The dragons have faces

The four dragons used to be four still pictures. They have animated
pixel sprites now:

- **Ice Dragon**: a young blue dragon
- **Thunder Dragon**: a serpentine wyvern
- **Fire Dragon**: an adult red
- **Void Dragon**: an ancient bone dragon

They only idle so far. The pack doesn't have attack, hurt or death animations
yet, and when it does they'll drop straight in.

## Damage numbers

A hit puts a number over whoever took it: red for HP lost, green for HP back.
It's the HP that actually changed, so an overkill shows what was left, not the
size of the swing, and a heal at full shows nothing. They're gone in under half
a second, before the next member acts, because a pile of leftover numbers read
like a combo system the game doesn't have.

## Two things the new layout broke

**The shop's shelves.** Gear, Supplies and Scrolls open into lists, and each
list used to be a little scroll box inside the page's own scroll. On a phone
the two fought over every swipe, and half the time the wrong one moved. An open
shelf lays everything out in the page now, and there's one scroll. The menu's
Items and Equipment tabs got the same fix.

**The affinity chart.** When the battle screen was turned round, the chart
under each enemy was told to shrink to its own width. It's drawn rather than
built from pieces, so it doesn't have a width of its own, and it came out zero
pixels wide. It was there the whole time, just invisible. It fills the card
again, so weaknesses and "?" are back under every enemy.

## Still on the list

Still no sound. Still nothing on the page that says up front it's built for a
phone. And the dragons need something to do besides breathe.

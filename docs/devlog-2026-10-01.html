Four dragons deep used to be the whole game. Beat the Void Dragon on floor 20 and the run was over. It isn’t over any more. There are five more floors under the last dragon, and something at the bottom that has been raising the dead the whole time.

The Abyss

Floors 21 to 24 are mazes with no warden and no boss corridor. Every floor lays all four hazards at once (more on those below), and a fight can pull a demon from any of the four tiers, built at Abyss depth, around level 32 to 38. Each roamer burns in a random band’s colour. The walls are bone white. Every other band already had a colour, and this is the one with none left.

The Necromancer

Floor 25 is a corridor like the dragons’ floors, with an orb at its mouth and the Necromancer at the far end. Beat it and the run is over.

It doesn’t fight like anything else:

  • Every phase it takes a new form: ice, thunder, fire or dark, never the one it just had. It wears that dragon’s affinity chart until its next phase and casts only that element, so the first spell it throws tells you what it is weak to. What you learn about each form is kept per form. Find its weak spot in ice form, and it’s still marked the next time it turns to ice.
  • It raises skeletons: one a phase, up to three standing, at half its level. Each fights with its own kind’s attacks and brings its own press-turn icon, so its two icons grow to five with a full guard. Kill it and they crumble with it.
  • It cleans up: Steady off its own side, or Purge off yours, at most once a phase and only when there’s something to clear.

For a day it was a purple-tinted Wizard. It has its own art now, from the same pack as everyone else: a hooded figure with a scythe and a staff. It casts with the staff and swings the scythe when it’s silenced. When it summons, a green circle opens on the floor and the skeleton climbs out of it. The skeletons in that pack all came with a rising animation, and this is the first time I’ve had a use for it.

Every band has its own floor

Each band of the dungeon has a hazard of its own now. None of them show up on warden or dragon floors.

  • Floors 1-5, ice: you slide along the corridor until you hit solid floor. Ice only goes on straight runs with solid floor at both ends, so a slide can never leave you stuck.
  • Floors 6-10, charged plates: two groups that swap every two steps. A lit plate costs 15% of your max HP. My first version pulsed in real time, and on a phone it was miserable. Now your steps are the clock. Turning and standing still don’t count, and if the next plate is lit, step off and back and it’s dark.
  • Floors 11-15, lava: pools of two to four tiles, about eight a floor. A crossing costs 15%, halved if you resist fire. A pool only goes down where the floor stays connected without it, so there’s always a way round.
  • Floors 16-20, teleporters: one or two two-way pairs, both ends in dead ends, so they only ever add a route.

Fire resistance halves the burn, and gear that nulls, repels or drains an element ignores its tiles completely.

Stairs that go down, and chests you can see

The way out used to be a flight of steps going up, which is an odd way out of a dungeon whose whole point is going deeper. It’s a stairwell now, six steps dropping into the dark. A staircase going down is hard to see from eye height, because the steps hide behind the lip of the floor. So the ceiling steps down with them, and it’s the upside-down staircase overhead that says "down". The green light comes up from the bottom of the flight.

Chests got the same treatment. They’re 3D now, planks and iron in an alcove cut into the wall. An opened one has its lid thrown back. Opening a chest shows what you found in a panel with a Close button, instead of one line at the top of the screen that was gone in two seconds.

The Mimic is gone. It was a chest that bit, and once chests started showing you what’s inside, it stopped being worth the trouble.

Ailments got rules

  • They wear off: every ailment lasts three of the victim’s own turns, whoever threw it. Poison bites three times. The log tells you when one lifts.
  • Enemies obey them: a silenced demon can’t cast, buff or leech, so it swings. Dragons and the Necromancer shrug off Silence and Blind four times in five.
  • Blind replaces Immobilize: it halves agility in a fight, so you miss more, dodge less, and have a harder time running. Bind became Blind, the Elixir of Motion became Eye Drops.
  • Paralysis skips half your turns now, up from a quarter.
  • You can see them: poison bubbles, sunglasses for Blind, a "..." bubble for Silence, yellow zig-zags for Paralysis, drawn right over the portrait. A dodged attack floats MISS like a damage number.

Five new trinkets ward them off: Star Pendant (poison), Silver Specs (blind), Echo Bangle (silence), Grounding Cord (paralysis), 400 gold each from floor 6. And the Ribbon, which wards all four. That one’s a Final Fantasy nod, and you can only find it.

A slot machine in the orb

Every orb has a Gacha tab. Pay, pull, three reels: experience, gold, an item, a monster. Two alike pays that face, three alike is the jackpot. A pair of chests is a supply, a jackpot of chests is gear from the next band down. A monster joins you at no higher than your own level.

It said EXP, GOLD, ITEM and MON at first. Those are the icons above now, drawn in the same 16px style as the element icons.

Gold on its own pays back about a third of what you put in, so it’s not a way to make money. That’s what the Gauntlet is for, which pays twice the lineup’s price now.

Luck has no ceiling

Luck used to only matter in a fight, and it stopped mattering at 30, where crits capped at 25%. Two changes:

  • The Gacha listens to it. Each point gives the second and third reels a half-point chance to land on a face that’s already showing. At 30 Luck a jackpot goes from 6 in 100 to about 13, and an empty spin from 38 to 27.
  • No caps. Crits and the Gacha both keep climbing. At 180 Luck every hit crits, and at 200 every spin is a jackpot. Good luck getting there.

Seeds

Rare drops: Seed of Strength, Defence, Wisdom, Agility and Luck, each one permanent point in that stat. 8% from a chest, 2% from a kill. Seeds and stones can go to any monster on the roster, not just the hero. Monsters have no Luck, though, so the Seed of Luck is yours.

Also in this one

  • Lists scroll when you drag anywhere on them, not just in the gaps between buttons.
  • Potions and Ethers ask who they’re for in a fight, so a monster’s MP can finally be topped up mid-fight.
  • The one-line note on the HUD moved off the minimap, where it was covering your own marker.
  • Each dragon stands at the far end of its corridor, in its idle animation, until you beat it.
  • From the phone playtest: the back of the stairwell showed through as a black band from the corridor behind it, and a long message in the menu pushed the buttons off the screen. Both fixed.

Still on the list

The Necromancer hasn’t had a proper run-through yet, so its numbers are a guess. Demons do come back on a cleared floor, but only one every 25 steps, and that may be too slow. And still no sound.

How a demon spends its turn

read the Attacks and Skill columns together

A demon checks three things in order and swings if none of them fire. Ailment goes out first, three turns in ten, and only while someone standing is still clean — it can be thrown at anyone on your side, not just whoever it is hitting. Skill is next, also three in ten. Attacks with lists the lines it can call up, and it reaches for one every turn it can pay: MP is a magazine, not a dice roll. A demon carrying several picks between them at random, so there is no single resistance that answers it. A banishing line is the exception, eligible only one turn in five, because a demon it takes from you does not come back. Demons marked never swings are casters — out of MP they scrape the dregs of their cheapest ordinary line at half strength rather than throwing a punch.

All three come out of the same pool, and a demon never spends its last MP on anything but its element. 35 of the 39 demons call up an element, 25 throw an ailment, and 25 carry a skill.

What the table says is missing

The tiers are lopsided. Ten templates cover tier I and ten cover tier II, but only six cover tier III and four cover tier IV. The deepest five floors — the ones a player only reaches by earning them — have the least to show. Filling those two bands is worth more than anything else you could add.

Every warden still needs a sprite. All five are written with an art note and none is drawn. They are the most drawable things on the list — a gargoyle, a wight, a hound, a basilisk and a mimic all have unmistakable silhouettes — so they are the sensible place to start.

Only four ailments exist, and two of them do the same job. Bind and Paralysis both cost a demon its turn, so across thirty-nine entries the real variety is poison, silence and “you do not act”. Now that throwing one costs a demon its turn, that thinness shows more than it used to.
